## Summary: ten decisions this research supports

1. **Position on method, not adjectives.** Lead with what a consultancy-led studio actually decides (plans, materials, coordination) and who decides it (the two founders). Avoid "luxury", "bespoke", "award-winning" unless the owner can substantiate them. ([Q1](#1-how-premium-studios-position-their-brands), [Q8](#8-building-trust-without-unsupported-claims))
2. **Call the portfolio what it is.** Label the three projects as design visualisations on the project page and in captions, and show the thinking behind each render (plan, zoning, material board). ([Q5](#5-how-a-portfolio-builds-credibility))
3. **Put real people on the page.** Real portraits and role-specific bios for both founders; no stock people anywhere. ([Q2](#2-how-prospective-clients-evaluate-interior-designers), [Q8](#8-building-trust-without-unsupported-claims))
4. **Sell six services as six purchasable decisions**, grouped by stage, each with "when it fits" and "what you receive". ([Q7](#7-how-services-should-be-presented))
5. **One contact intent, one short form, plus WhatsApp and email.** Five or six fields, single column, labels above inputs, consent line for UAE data protection. ([Q9](#9-the-discovery-to-enquiry-path))
6. **Trust from verifiable signals only.** Legal entity, named founders, process, deliverable samples and third-party profiles. No testimonials, logos or star ratings until they exist. ([Q8](#8-building-trust-without-unsupported-claims))
7. **Image rules:** wide view, mid view and detail for every space; scale cues; one colour grade per project; informative captions. ([Q10](#10-photography-material-detail-spatial-composition-and-typography))
8. **Use the brand colours where they pass contrast.** Oxblood text on sand is 9.74:1 and on blush 7.79:1; white text on blush or sand fails. ([Q10](#10-photography-material-detail-spatial-composition-and-typography))
9. **Motion like walking through a building:** short reveals and one cross-page transition, no scrolljacking, no auto-rotating carousels, everything off under reduced-motion settings. ([Q12](#12-how-restrained-animation-can-serve-an-architectural-experience))
10. **Local SEO without doorway pages.** Google Business Profile first; Organization markup now; LocalBusiness markup only once a real, staffed address is confirmed; one genuine page per service and per project, not one page per Dubai neighbourhood. ([Q14](#14-seo-for-local-high-intent-clients-in-dubai))

## 1. How premium studios position their brands

**Findings**

- **[S 9, S 10, S 13, S 14]** The strongest international studios say almost nothing on the home page. Pierre Yovanovitch and Vincent Van Duysen show a name and imagery with no tagline; Commune and Workstead use a plain gateway of three or four links. Their positioning lives in the projects and the About page.
- **[S 3, S 4, S 6]** Dubai studios lead with scale: Roar shows a stats strip ("13+ years, 350+ projects, 12+ countries"); Kristina Zanic Consultants shows counters (300+ projects, 200 designers, 26 countries); Antonovich's H1 is "Design, Build & Fit-Out luxury homes in Dubai & worldwide" beside "20+ years" and "500+ projects". Bluehaus calls itself a "market leading multi-disciplinary" practice **[S 1]**.
- **[S 3, S 4, S 15]** The same phrase recurs: "we create spaces that tell stories" (Roar), "designing spaces that tell stories" (Waldo), "Spaces with Stories" (Kristina Zanic).
- **[S 3, S 4, S 7]** Stated numbers drift: Roar's home page says "12+ countries" while its projects page counter says "160+"; Kristina Zanic's portfolio meta says 20 countries and its About page 26; Norm Architects says "15 years" next to "Founded in 2008".

- **[F 23]** NN/g's long-running trust research lists design quality as one of four credibility factors: clear organisation, visuals and colour that fit the business, and no errors. It notes that white space makes content seem better organised and that vague, "clever or nondescript" navigation labels make users doubt that content exists.
- **[F 25]** The aesthetic-usability effect: users perceive attractive products as more usable, but attractiveness only buys tolerance for minor problems, and the effect is strongest when aesthetics support the content.
- **[F 43]** Consumer research on white space in advertising (Pracejus, Olsen and O'Guinn, *Journal of Consumer Research*, 2006) argues that white space signals meaning learned through art history and culture, and that both creative directors and ordinary readers share that reading; related work by the same team reports creative directors use it partly to convey brand prestige. (Abstract-level source; full text is paywalled.)
- **[I]** In this sector, "premium" is communicated by restraint and specificity: one point of view, named people, few words, large and careful imagery. Superlatives ("luxury", "world-class", "bespoke") are cheap to write and are used by studios at every price level, so they carry little signal.
- **[I]** A young studio cannot borrow the signals of scale (hundreds of projects, decades, awards). Its premium position has to come from clarity of method and quality of presentation, which are both fully in its control. The international studios above show that a premium position can rest on work and voice alone; the Dubai studios show that scale numbers become a liability once they go stale.

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- Write the positioning as two plain sentences: what ARCHIÓ is (an interior design consultancy in Dubai for homes, workplaces, mixed-use and landscape) and how it works (design decisions made and documented before anything is built). Final wording is a strategy-phase task and needs owner approval.
- Make "consultancy-led" the differentiator. Many Dubai firms sell design-and-build fit-out packages; ARCHIÓ's service list is advisory and coordination work. State that difference plainly once the owner confirms whether ARCHIÓ ever executes works.
- Name both founders on the home page, not only on About.
- Use the trading name consistently ("Archió Interior Design Studio") and show the legal relationship ("a unit of Beracah Trading") in the footer and About page.
- Treat the accented name as a search problem as well as a brand asset: visitors will type "Archio". Use `alternateName` in structured data ([Q14](#14-seo-for-local-high-intent-clients-in-dubai)) and make sure the wordmark has a text equivalent.
- Ban list for positioning copy: "luxury", "bespoke", "timeless", "elevate", "curated", "award-winning", "leading", "#1", "spaces that tell stories", unless a specific, checkable fact stands behind the words.
- No numbers on the home page at launch. If numbers are used later, keep each one in a single content source so it cannot contradict itself across pages ([S 3], [S 4], [S 7]).

## 2. How prospective clients evaluate interior designers

**Findings**

- **[F 34]** Houzz (platform-owned research, US, reported in 2020) says reviews were homeowners' top criterion when choosing a professional, ahead of cost and of experience with their design style. The same piece advises posting the work most similar to the clients you want first, and notes homeowners "often spend 6-12 months doing research". Caveat: Houzz sells review-based profiles, so it has a commercial interest in this finding.
- **[F 35]** The 2026 U.S. Houzz & Home Study (covering 2025) shows planning takes longer than building: for kitchens, 9.5 months of planning against 5.8 months of construction; for primary bathrooms, 8.4 against 4.8.
- **[F 36]** The British Institute of Interior Design's client guidance says the key is finding a designer with "appropriate experience, competence, sympathy with your vision" and rigorous working practices; its client guide covers how to appoint a designer, fees, and the process from start to finish (UK professional body).
- **[F 23]** NN/g: for service businesses, showing the actual work and the people doing it builds more trust than generic end-result images; sites should represent the full range of what is offered so a visitor does not feel like "a second-rate customer"; third-party reviews are trusted more than testimonials on the company's own site.
- **[F 33]** The Stanford Web Credibility guidelines (based on research with over 4,500 people) include showing that there is a real organisation behind the site, highlighting expertise, showing honest and trustworthy people, and making it easy to make contact.
- **[I]** Combining these, a prospect's evaluation runs roughly in this order: (1) does their taste match mine, (2) have they handled a space like mine, (3) can I trust them, (4) how does working with them run and how is it charged, (5) how do I start. Each step needs an obvious answer on the site.

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- No reviews or testimonials are known to exist yet, which removes the strongest single signal in [F 34] until they do. Compensate with steps (2) to (4): specific project facts, visible founders, and a transparent process and fee **model** (how fees are structured, not invented prices). Ask the owner how fees are actually charged.
- Every project page answers step (2) in its first screen: category, community, size, scope ARCHIÓ delivered, and status.
- Order the three projects by the client the studio most wants next, per [F 34]. That ordering is an owner decision.
- Set up a Google Business Profile now so the first real clients have somewhere independent to leave reviews, and link to it from the site rather than copying reviews onto the site ([F 23], [Q8](#8-building-trust-without-unsupported-claims)).
- Assume a long research window ([F 34], [F 35]): give visitors things to return to (projects, a short guide to Dubai approvals, the process page) rather than pushing for contact on first visit.

## 3. What residential clients expect

**Findings**

- **[F 35]** In the 2026 U.S. Houzz & Home Study, interior rooms were the most common renovation category (67% of respondents' 2025 renovation activity), and planning periods are long (see [Q2](#2-how-prospective-clients-evaluate-interior-designers)). US data; used here only as a directional signal of how homeowners behave.
- **[F 31]** Baymard's e-commerce testing found 42% of users try to judge a product's size from its images and 56% start a product page by exploring images; without a scale reference, people draw wrong conclusions and abandon. **[I]** The same need appears in interiors: a homeowner wants to know whether a design fits a home their size.
- **[F 41]** Dubai recorded over 270,000 real estate sales transactions worth AED 917 billion in 2025 (up 20%), with 129,600 new investors. **[I]** A large, continuing flow of new owners and investors receiving handovers is the residential audience; many are expatriates and some are overseas, so they need remote-friendly communication and a clearly explained process.
- **[F 39]** Emaar Community Management's home-modification guidance says owners apply for a modification NOC through the community portal, that alterations need Emaar's approval, and that exterior changes altering villa aesthetics are not permitted. (Page located via search; it returned HTTP 403 to direct fetching, so the wording was confirmed only from the search index.)
- **[I]** Residential clients in Dubai expect: a taste match; a believable sense of how they will live in the space (plans and flow, not only beauty shots); materials that suit heat, humidity, sand and air-conditioning; help with building or community approvals; quick, informal communication; and an honest view of cost before commitment.

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- Residential page structure: what ARCHIÓ does in a home (by service), how a home project runs (stages and deliverables), approvals (what ARCHIÓ prepares for building or community NOCs, **only if** Project Coordination Support covers this; confirm with owner), and the Dubai Marina project as the lead example.
- Dubai Marina project page: include the floor plan or a zoning diagram if one exists in the asset inventory, and room-by-room views that show scale (people-height viewpoints, recognisable furniture), not only atmospheric crops.
- Write one practical guide: "Before you renovate an apartment or villa in Dubai: the approvals to expect", linking to the official authority pages. It answers a real question, demonstrates competence, and links out ([F 5], [F 23], [F 33]).
- Offer WhatsApp as a first-class contact route alongside the form ([Q9](#9-the-discovery-to-enquiry-path)).

## 4. What commercial and mixed-use clients expect

**Findings**

- **[F 37]** Gensler's Global Workplace Survey 2025 (over 16,000 office workers, 15 countries): workers spend about 55% of the week in the office but say they need about 65% to do their best work; only 26% strongly agree their workplace helps them do their best work; only 14% want a "corporate" workplace experience; 40% prioritise natural light and operable windows and 36% want greenery; employees in great workplaces are nearly three times more likely to stay.
- **[F 38]** Dubai Development Authority's fit-out permit requires a contractor appointment letter from the tenant, an NOC from the building owner, and approved stamped and proposed drawings; adding structural elements, changing the use or adding floor area needs a separate building modification request. Published fee: AED 0.90 per sq ft (minimum AED 200, maximum AED 10,000); published time: 2 working days.
- **[F 45]** DMCC, which administers the free zone that includes JLT, publishes its own fit-out NOC process for licensed companies through its member portal, addressed to the fit-out contractor. (Official DMCC form located via search; the host was unreachable on 25 October 2026, so details were confirmed only from the search index.)
- **[I]** Approvals in Dubai depend on who administers the plot (Dubai Municipality, DDA, DMCC, Trakhees and others), so a commercial client's real question is "will your drawings get through my landlord and authority without delays?"
- **[I]** Commercial buyers (owners, office managers, landlords) are buying reduced risk: a plan that fits headcount and ways of working, a brand-consistent environment, a predictable programme with minimal downtime, controlled budget, and clean coordination with contractors. Evidence of process and documentation persuades them more than mood imagery.
- **[I]** Mixed-use clients (developers, operators) add layered audiences (residents, tenants, visitors), durable specification, consistent identity across zones, and wayfinding.

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- Commercial page: lead with outcomes stated plainly (a space plan that fits the team, a materials specification, coordination with the fit-out contractor), then the JLT project with a plan or zoning diagram and a short "brief → response" story.
- Add a "What we need from you" checklist for commercial enquiries (lease or title, building fit-out rules, headcount, move-in date). It is useful content and pre-qualifies enquiries.
- Use Gensler-type findings carefully: cite them as context for design choices (daylight, greenery, non-corporate atmosphere) on the commercial page or a journal piece, with the source named.
- **Mixed-use has no project in the portfolio.** Do not imply one. Either present mixed-use as a capability in one paragraph on the services page, or leave it out of the main navigation until a real project exists. Landscape does have a project (Arabian Ranches 2) and can stand as its own category.

## 5. How a portfolio builds credibility

**Findings**

- **[S 16]** Neri&Hu's project pages have an Information tab with Gross Area, **Status** ("Complete"), Completion Date, Duration and Address (checked: Telegraph Hotel, 30,608 sqm, January 2021 to June 2025).
- **[S 7, S 10, S 11, S 15]** Other strong project pages carry structured facts and credits: Norm Architects (location, named photographer, category, year, about 450 words, 85 images); Vincent Van Duysen ("Photos by" credit, publications list); Kelly Wearstler (location, type, year, square footage); Waldo (client, services, photography credit, attributed press quotes).
- **[S 1, S 5, S 6, S 12]** Weak project pages: Sneha Divias Atelier shows a title, one sentence and a location; Studio McGee one paragraph; Antonovich SEO prose without location, size or year; Bluehaus has no public portfolio, only a credentials PDF gated behind name and email.
- **[S 2, S 4, S 13]** Sector filters are the norm (for example Kristina Zanic: All, Hospitality, F&B, Wellness, Corporate, Residential).
- **[S 7, S 10, S 14, S 16]** Unbuilt work: no inspected studio captioned a render as a render on the pages opened. Honest structural devices do exist: Neri&Hu's "Status" field, Workstead's "In Progress" project filter, Vincent Van Duysen's "Competition" category, and Norm's awards list that labels entries "longlist", "shortlist" or "nomination". Studios that mention 3D work present it as a deliverable (Sneha Divias, Antonovich, Studio McGee) **[S 5, S 6, S 12]**. A sample of 16 sites does not prove no one captions renders.

- **[F 24]** NN/g eyetracking: users scrutinise images that carry information and ignore purely decorative ones; when users ask for a larger view, the enlargement should be "at least twice as big".
- **[F 31]** Baymard: provide at least one image that shows scale; computer-generated scale images can cover large catalogues but "should supplement real photography, not replace it".
- **[F 34]** Houzz advises descriptive project titles (project type, design style and one other attribute) and both wide and close-up shots.
- **[F 23]** NN/g: show the process, not only generic end results.
- **[F 5]** Google's people-first content guidance asks whether it is clear who made content, how, and why, and asks whether any use of automation, including AI generation, is "self-evident to visitors through disclosures or in other ways".
- **[F 42]** The IPTC Digital Source Type vocabulary (used by Google for image provenance) distinguishes "Digital creation: media created by a human using non-generative tools" from "Created using Generative AI" (`trainedAlgorithmicMedia`) and composites containing generative AI (`compositeSynthetic`).

### Presenting ARCHIÓ's visualisations credibly

The honest position is that ARCHIÓ's three projects are 3D design visualisations, not completed and photographed builds.

- **[I]** The risk is not that visitors see renders; Dubai buyers see off-plan renders constantly. The risk is a visitor assuming they are photographs and later discovering they are not. Upfront disclosure is one of NN/g's four trust factors ([F 23]); a late discovery undoes it.
- **[I]** Handled well, visualisations are evidence of exactly what a consultancy sells: thinking, planning and specification before money is spent on site.

Recommended treatment:

1. **Label at project level and per image.** A fact line on every project ("Status: Design visualisation", following the precedent of Neri&Hu's Status field [S 16]) and a short caption on the first image of each project. Legible size, not grey fine print. None of the 16 inspected studios had a visible render label, so there is no convention to follow **[S 1 to S 16]**; ARCHIÓ can set a clear one.
2. **Name the section accurately.** "Work" or "Design studies", not "Completed projects". When built work arrives, a status filter (like Workstead's "In Progress" [S 14]) keeps visualised and built work apart rather than mixing them silently.
3. **Show what sits behind the render:** floor plan or zoning diagram, material and finish board with named finishes, a page of specification. This converts a pretty picture into proof of method ([F 23], [F 24]).
4. **State ARCHIÓ's scope** on each project (which of the six services were delivered).
5. **Say whether it was a client commission** only once the owner confirms. If it was a self-initiated study, say so; that is still a legitimate showcase.
6. **Embed provenance metadata** in image files: IPTC Digital Source Type `digitalCreation` for human-made 3D renders. If any generative AI was used in an image, disclose it on the page and use `trainedAlgorithmicMedia` or `compositeSynthetic` as appropriate ([F 5], [F 42]).
7. **Never** add fake photographer credits, AI-generated rooms presented as ARCHIÓ's design, or imagery from other studios.
8. **Plan the upgrade path:** when the first project is built, photograph it and show the visualisation beside the built result. Design-versus-built is one of the strongest credibility devices available to a consultancy **[I]**.

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- Project page template (all three projects): title, facts block (category, community, area, scope, status, year if known), lead image, 3 to 5 short story sections each tied to images ([Q6](#6-how-project-storytelling-communicates-expertise)), plan or diagram, material board, credits.
- Each space shown as wide, mid and detail views; enlargements open at least twice the inline size ([F 24]).
- The portfolio index is a small, curated set; three projects are enough if each page is deep. Do not pad it with mood images.

## 6. How project storytelling communicates expertise

**Findings**

- **[S 2, S 3, S 4, S 9]** Many project narratives are short: LW Design about 100 words; Roar a brief plus approach of about 120 words; Kristina Zanic about 110 words in two or three headed blocks; Pierre Yovanovitch about 140 words on an image-led page of 72 photographs.
- **[S 7, S 10, S 11, S 13]** The richer ones run 300 to 450 words and mix narrative with checkable specifics: Commune states the house was built in 1959, is 3,100 sq ft and names the timber maker; Norm gives approximate area and names the furniture maker; Kelly Wearstler names artists and makers; Vincent Van Duysen adds a publications list.

- **[F 5]** Google's self-assessment asks whether content shows "first-hand expertise and a depth of knowledge" and whether it was written or reviewed by someone who demonstrably knows the topic.
- **[F 23]** NN/g: sharing expertise and showing the whole process signal authority.
- **[F 34]** Houzz: titles should describe the project type, style and a distinguishing attribute.
- **[I]** Expertise shows in specific, checkable decisions (orientation, circulation, a joinery detail, why a stone was chosen for a sun-exposed terrace), not in adjectives. A reader should finish a project page able to say what the designer decided and why.

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- Use one story structure for every project: **Premise** (the brief, or for a self-initiated study, the question being explored) → **Constraints** (size, light, views, climate, building rules) → **Key moves** (at most three decisions, each with an image) → **Materials** (named finishes and why) → **What the client receives** (deliverables).
- Keep each project to roughly 300 to 600 words of body text, broken into short sections. Captions are functional ("Living room looking west towards the marina") rather than poetic.
- Write in the founders' voice and credit them by name on each project; if visualisation work was outsourced, credit the visualiser (owner to confirm).
- For the Arabian Ranches 2 landscape project, explain the climate logic (shade, planting, irrigation, heat on hard surfaces) because that knowledge is specific and checkable. **[I]**

## 7. How services should be presented

**Findings**

- **[S 1, S 2, S 4]** Large Dubai practices describe services thinly: Bluehaus in three paragraphs of about 40 words with no process; LW Design in two discipline pages with no process; Kristina Zanic in one sentence on About plus an Approach page citing ISO 9001.
- **[S 5]** Sneha Divias Atelier names its deliverables, including "technical drawings, material specifications and 3D renders".
- **[S 6]** Antonovich (design-and-build) shows a five-step process: Consultation ("space, budget and timeline"), Design (including 3D visualisation), Approvals, Fit-out, Handover.
- **[S 12]** Studio McGee shows a four-step process (Discovery; Concept; Design & Documentation, including renderings; Procurement & Installation) and sells three tiers: Full-Service, Virtual Design and Design Concierge.

- **[F 23]** NN/g: disclose service expectations up front; users dislike lengthy quote forms; price ranges are acceptable.
- **[F 36]** The BIID client guide treats fees and the process "from start to finish" as core information clients need before appointing a designer.
- **[I]** For a consultancy, the services are the product. Six equal icon cards in a grid is the most common, least informative way to show them and gives a visitor no help choosing. Studio McGee's tiers are the closest model to ARCHIÓ's separately bookable services, and Antonovich shows that Dubai clients expect approvals to be named as a step.

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- Group the six services by stage (proposed; owner to confirm the fit):
  - **Define:** Interior Design Consultation, Space Planning
  - **Design:** Concept Development, Material & Finish Selection
  - **Support:** Design Review & Advisory, Project Coordination Support
- For each service, give: what it is (one sentence), when it fits, what the client receives, what the client provides, and whether it can be booked on its own. "Design Review & Advisory" in particular can serve clients who already have a contractor or another designer, which is a distinctive offer for a consultancy. **[I]**
- State what ARCHIÓ does not do (for example, contracting or supplying furniture) once the owner confirms, so mismatched enquiries filter themselves out.
- Publish the fee model if the owner is willing (for example fixed fee per stage, or per square foot); no invented numbers.
- One services page with anchored sections is enough at launch. Separate service pages are justified later only if each has substantial unique content ([F 4]).

## 8. Building trust without unsupported claims

**Findings**

- **[F 33]** Stanford's credibility guidelines: make information verifiable, show a real organisation, highlight expertise, show honest people, make contact easy, look professional, keep content current, use restraint with promotion, avoid errors of every kind.
- **[F 23]** NN/g: external presence (reviews, social, news) matters because isolated sites seem to have "something to hide"; third-party reviews beat on-site testimonials; linking out to reviews shows confidence; typos and broken links "quickly degrade credibility".
- **[F 3]** Google: when an entity controls the reviews about itself, its LocalBusiness or Organization pages are not eligible for review stars in search. **[I]** Review markup on ARCHIÓ's own site would therefore gain nothing.
- **[F 5]** Google's E-E-A-T guidance: "Of these aspects, trust is most important."
- **[F 24]** Real staff portraits draw attention; generic stock photos are ignored.
- **[F 44]** The UAE's Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on personal data "prohibits the processing of personal data without the consent of the individual" unless an exception applies (for example performing a contract or meeting a legal obligation); consent must be "specific, clear and unambiguous"; marketing needs an opt-out; and, unlike GDPR, there is no "legitimate interests" basis. Free zones with their own regimes (DIFC, ADGM) are excluded. (Law text on the UAE legislation portal; the summary relied on here is a Clyde & Co briefing from November 2021. Have the owner's adviser confirm current obligations, since executive regulations may have changed details.)
- **[I]** Verifiable signals ARCHIÓ can show today: the legal entity, a trade licence number (if provided), two named founders with real portraits, real contact details, a written process, sample deliverables, Dubai-specific knowledge, and consistent profiles on Google, Instagram and LinkedIn.

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- **Do:** founder portraits and bios built only from confirmed facts; entity line and licence number; privacy notice and a consent line on the enquiry form ([F 44]); a redacted sample deliverable (for example one page of a finish schedule); links out to the Google Business Profile and social profiles; a visible "last updated" date on guides.
- **Do not:** years in business, project counters, "trusted by" logo walls, award badges, press bars, star ratings, anonymous or first-name-only testimonials, stock photos of people, AggregateRating or Review markup on ARCHIÓ's own pages ([F 3]).
- When real testimonials exist: full name (or initials with permission), project type and community, permission recorded, and preferably the review lives on Google with the site linking to it ([F 23]).
- Proofread every page and check every link before launch ([F 23], [F 33]).

## 9. The discovery-to-enquiry path

**Findings**

- **[F 40]** At the start of 2025 the UAE had 11.1 million internet users (99% penetration) and 11.3 million social media user identities; Instagram's ad reach was 7.6 million (67.8% of the population), YouTube's 8.25 million, and LinkedIn reported 9.4 million registered members (members, not active users).
- **[F 9]** Google ranks local results on relevance, distance and prominence; prominence includes links and reviews; "More reviews and positive ratings can help"; there is "no way to request or pay for a better local ranking".
- **[F 27]** NN/g: hidden (hamburger) navigation on desktop was used by 27% of participants versus 48% for visible navigation, and desktop tasks took at least 39% longer with hidden navigation.
- **[F 26]** NN/g form guidance: keep forms short, single column, labels close to fields, avoid placeholder text, mark optional fields and keep them to one or two, explain format requirements, and give specific, visible errors that preserve input.
- **[F 32]** Baymard (e-commerce checkout, 2024): the average checkout has 11.3 form fields while most sites need only 8; 17% of users have abandoned a checkout because of its complexity. **[I]** An enquiry form has less excuse for length than a checkout.
- **[F 23]** NN/g: avoid gates (asking for personal data before showing value) and lengthy quote forms.
- **[I]** WhatsApp is widely used for business contact in the UAE, but the DataReportal page does not publish a WhatsApp figure and no primary statistic was found; validate with the owner's own enquiry history.

- **[S 7, S 8, S 9, S 10, S 15]** Many top international studios have no enquiry form at all, only role-based emails (new business, press, careers) and a phone number; Studio Ashby adds a separate "New business enquiries" address and a WhatsApp link.
- **[S 2, S 4, S 5]** Dubai studios that have forms keep them short and unqualified: LW Design asks name, email, phone, studio and message; Kristina Zanic asks name, email, phone, subject and message (all three contact fields required); Sneha Divias repeats a four-field form in every footer.
- **[S 12, S 14]** The strongest qualifying paths: Workstead asks project type, location, timeline stage (Planning, In Design, In Construction, Ready to Start, Flexible) and how the visitor heard, under the button "Start the Conversation"; Studio McGee runs a multi-step form (service tier, location, approximate size, timeframes, construction budget, aligned past projects, Pinterest link, reason) and promises a reply in "2-3 business days".
- **[S 1, S 3]** Gates: Bluehaus asks for name and email before releasing its credentials PDF; Roar's contact form ends in a portfolio PDF download. Both are the kind of gate NN/g warns against **[F 23]**.
- **[S 6]** Only one of the six Dubai studios inspected showed WhatsApp: Antonovich, which also puts four calls to action in its hero (Book Consultation, WhatsApp Us, Call, Private Palace Inquiry), a floating WhatsApp button and a "within one business day" reply promise.

**Likely paths [I]:** Instagram post or reel → profile link → project page; Google Maps or search ("interior designer Dubai Marina") → Business Profile → home or project; referral → founder name or brand search → About → contact.

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- Visible top navigation on desktop with four or five items (for example Work, Services, Studio, Journal later, Contact); a menu button is acceptable on mobile, but keep the contact action visible there ([F 27]).
- One contact intent and one label for it across the site (nav, project pages, footer), per the design-taste guidance that duplicate CTA labels for the same intent read as template filler. Project pages can pass the category to the form through the link so the visitor does not have to choose again.
- Enquiry form (draft): name; email or phone (visitor chooses one, one required); project type (Residential, Commercial, Mixed-use, Landscape as radio buttons); community or location; project stage (optional, Workstead-style: Planning, In design, Ready to start [S 14]); short message, where size and timing can go. Consent checkbox with a link to the privacy notice ([F 44]). Six inputs, one of them optional ([F 26], [F 32]).
- Budget question: leave it out at launch. Add an optional budget band only if the owner wants stronger qualification and accepts fewer enquiries, as Studio McGee does [S 12] **[I]**.
- No gated downloads: nothing a visitor wants (portfolio, credentials) sits behind a form ([F 23], [S 1], [S 3]).
- Alongside the form: a WhatsApp click-to-chat link, the email address, and the phone number, all real and monitored. Show WhatsApp as a quiet link in the contact block and footer, not a floating bubble; among the Dubai studios inspected, only the sales-led design-and-build firm used one [S 6] **[I]**.
- After submission: say what happens next and when. Only promise a response time the studio will reliably meet.
- Measure form submissions and WhatsApp clicks, with analytics loaded according to the consent approach the owner's adviser approves.

## 10. Photography, material detail, spatial composition and typography

**Findings**

- **[S 4, S 7, S 11]** Classic serif headlines over cream are common: Kristina Zanic (a serif display face with Avant Garde body on `#FFFEF9`), Norm Architects (Caslon with Euclid on cream), Kelly Wearstler (Caslon on cream).
- **[S 6, S 9, S 14, S 16]** Others are sans-only: Pierre Yovanovitch (Surt on ivory), Workstead (Gotham Narrow, black and white), Neri&Hu (Helvetica Neue), Antonovich (Jost and Hanken Grotesk on warm off-white).
- **[S 7, S 8, S 10, S 15]** Credited photography is a mark of the strongest studios: Norm, Vincent Van Duysen and Waldo name the photographer; Studio Ashby names photographer and stylist. Image counts per project are high (Norm 85, Commune 77, Pierre Yovanovitch 72) **[S 7, S 9, S 13]**.

- **[F 24]** Users study images that inform and skip decorative ones; NN/g recommends investing in quality images that serve the visitor's task.
- **[F 31]** Images should show scale; users start with images.
- **[F 34]** Wide and close-up shots; homeowners like to see details.
- **[F 6]** Google Images: use sharp, high-quality images, descriptive file names (not `IMG00023.JPG`), accurate alt text in context, place images near relevant text, and use real `<img>` elements because CSS background images are not indexed.
- **[F 22]** W3C WAI: informative images need alt text that conveys their meaning; purely decorative images take an empty `alt`.
- **[F 21]** WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 (Level AA): body text needs at least 4.5:1 contrast and large text (at least 18pt, or 14pt bold) at least 3:1; logotypes are exempt.
- **[C]** Contrast of the brand palette, computed with the WCAG relative-luminance formula:

| Text colour | Background | Ratio | Body text (4.5:1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oxblood `#4C1114` | Sand `#DACFBA` | 9.74:1 | Pass (also AAA) |
| Oxblood `#4C1114` | Blush `#CEB6A8` | 7.79:1 | Pass (also AAA) |
| Oxblood `#4C1114` | White `#FFFFFF` | 15.03:1 | Pass |
| Off-black `#1C1C1C` | Sand `#DACFBA` | 11.05:1 | Pass |
| White `#FFFFFF` | Blush `#CEB6A8` | 1.93:1 | **Fail**, even for large text |

| Blush `#CEB6A8` | Sand `#DACFBA` | 1.25:1 | Too close to separate regions or show focus |

- **[F 15]** web.dev: every web font has a cost; prefer WOFF2, subset to the needed glyphs, reduce the number of font files, and use `size-adjust` on fallbacks to reduce layout shift.
- **[I]** Interior imagery reads as professional when vertical lines stay vertical (two-point perspective), cameras sit near eye height, light direction is consistent with the room's orientation, and each space is shown as a sequence: establishing view, relationship between zones, then material detail. Material close-ups should show texture, joints and edges, because that is where quality is judged.
- **[I]** Type carries the brand's voice as much as imagery does. An Optima-style flared humanist face reads architectural and classical; Public Sans is neutral and readable; Lora is a book serif suited to long reading. Because serif-on-cream is now the most common premium-interiors look in the sample, an Optima-style display face is itself a point of difference, provided Lora does not take over the headlines.

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- **Image direction for renders:** correct verticals, eye-height viewpoints, one consistent colour grade per project, natural light logic, and at least one view per space with a scale cue (furniture, a door, a person-height reference). Retire any render with visible artefacts.
- **Material detail:** give every project a material board (photographed samples if possible, otherwise clean swatches) with finish names, so materials are named, not just shown.
- **Type roles (proposal):** Optima substitute for display and headings only; Public Sans for navigation, UI, captions and body; Lora reserved for one role at most (for example long-form project narrative or journal body), never mixed into headlines. Fewer families means fewer files ([F 15]).
- **Colour use:** oxblood or off-black for text on sand and blush; never white text on blush or sand; use oxblood as a full field (for example the footer or a statement section) with white or sand text on it rather than as thin accents scattered everywhere. Do not rely on blush-versus-sand alone to separate areas or to show focus.
- **Image SEO and accessibility:** descriptive file names such as `dubai-marina-apartment-living-room-visualisation.avif`, alt text that describes the space and states it is a visualisation where relevant, images placed beside the text that discusses them ([F 6], [F 22]).

## 11. Whitespace and hierarchy on premium sites

**Findings**

- **[S 7, S 9, S 10]** The reputation-led international studios use very little copy and a lot of space: Norm's home is imagery with small-caps navigation; Pierre Yovanovitch's home is near-empty and type-led; Vincent Van Duysen shows one full-screen image with no text.
- **[S 2, S 6]** Dubai sites in the sample are denser: LW Design's home is text-heavy with small justified type; Antonovich's home is about 24,000 px tall with 241 images and a mega-menu of about 250 links.

- **[F 23]** White space makes content seem better organised; meaningful labels beat clever ones.
- **[F 43]** White space carries a learned association with prestige in advertising.
- **[F 25]** Attractiveness forgives only minor usability problems; "form and function should work together".
- **[F 7]** Google recommends a prominent, distinctive main heading (such as the first visible `<h1>`) and descriptive, distinct page titles.
- **[I]** Premium white space is structured, not empty: a consistent grid and margins, one focal element per screen, a short type scale where hierarchy comes from size, weight and colour together, and grouping by proximity instead of boxes and cards. Unstructured emptiness reads as unfinished.
- **[I]** Density tracks the business model: sales-led design-and-build sites pack in links and calls to action, reputation-led studios rely on silence. ARCHIÓ lacks the reputation that lets Vincent Van Duysen say nothing, so it needs more explanation than those studios, but that explanation belongs on deeper pages (services, process, projects), not on a crowded home page.

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- Grid: 12 columns on desktop with generous outer margins, collapsing to a single column on phones; images may break the grid deliberately, text never does.
- Type scale of about five steps; body line length around 60 to 70 characters.
- One focal element per viewport on key pages (a project image, a statement, or the contact action), never several competing.
- Group with space and occasional hairlines rather than cards, shadows and coloured boxes.
- Every page gets one clear `<h1>` that says what the page is about in plain words ([F 7]).

## 12. How restrained animation can serve an architectural experience

**Findings**

- **[F 19]** WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (Level AAA): motion triggered by interaction can be disabled unless essential; non-essential movement, including scroll-triggered movement and parallax, can cause dizziness, nausea and headaches for people with vestibular disorders. The `prefers-reduced-motion` media query is a sufficient technique.
- **[F 20]** WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (Level A): moving or scrolling content that starts automatically and lasts more than five seconds needs a way to pause, stop or hide it.
- **[F 28]** NN/g (2023): scrolljacking disorients users, frustrates goal-driven visitors, is worse on mobile, and can hurt brand perception; if used at all, use it below the fold, keep it short, keep vertical direction, avoid it on mobile, and keep important content out of it. Conclusion: it "leads to more user pain than benefits".
- **[F 29]** NN/g: most UI animations should last 100 to 500 ms, typically 100 to 400 ms; 500 ms starts to drag; entering elements slightly longer than exiting; ease-out for entering elements; respect reduce-motion settings.
- **[F 30]** NN/g: auto-forwarding carousels reduce visibility and make users feel they have lost control; change panels only when users ask.
- **[F 16]** Chrome: CSS scroll-driven animations stay smooth under main-thread load where JavaScript scroll-event animations become janky (support listed: Chrome and Edge 115, Safari 26; Firefox in preview).
- **[F 17]** Chrome: cross-document view transitions between pages are supported in Chrome and Edge 126 and Safari 18.2, not yet in Firefox.
- **[I]** Architecture is experienced in sequence: thresholds, reveals, light changing across a room. Motion on an interiors site earns its place when it reproduces that sequence (revealing, transitioning, focusing) and loses it when it decorates (floating, spinning, following the cursor).

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- **Allowed:** image reveals (opacity or a soft mask) of 300 to 400 ms with ease-out, played once; one cross-page transition from a project thumbnail into the project hero (enhancement where supported, ordinary navigation elsewhere); subtle hover states on project links; CSS scroll-driven effects only where they clarify sequence.
- **Not allowed:** scrolljacking, horizontal scroll hijacks, auto-rotating hero carousels, parallax on text, custom cursors, magnetic buttons, looping marquees of service names.
- **Hero video:** only if the owner has a strong clip; muted, short, with a poster image and a visible pause control ([F 20]); never the only way to see key content.
- **Reduced motion:** every animation off or reduced to a simple fade when `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is set ([F 19]).

## 13. Keeping usability and performance with rich visuals

**Findings**

- **[F 12]** Core Web Vitals "good" thresholds, assessed at the 75th percentile of page loads: LCP 2.5 s or less, INP 200 ms or less, CLS 0.1 or less.
- **[F 13]** web.dev on LCP: make the LCP image discoverable in the initial HTML, set `fetchpriority="high"` on it, "Never lazy-load your LCP image", serve appropriately sized AVIF or WebP, and avoid client-side rendering of the LCP element.
- **[F 14]** web.dev: lazy-load only images outside the initial viewport, and give every `<img>` width and height so space is reserved and layout does not shift.
- **[F 18]** HTTP Archive Web Almanac 2024 (mobile): 9.5% of LCP images are wrongly lazy-loaded; `srcset` appears on 42% of pages but `sizes` errors affect image selection on 14% of mobile pages; AVIF is only 1% of images; the 90th-percentile largest image is about 1 MB; 23% of `<video>` elements autoplay.
- **[F 15]** Fonts: WOFF2, subsetting, fewer files, `size-adjust` fallbacks.
- **[F 6]** Use `<img>` (with `srcset` or `<picture>` and a `src` fallback) rather than CSS backgrounds for images that should be indexed.
- **[F 27]** Hidden desktop navigation lowers discoverability and slows tasks.
- **[S 1, S 7, S 10, S 16]** Several respected studios ship very heavy home pages (one-off readings in a 1440 px browser, not benchmarks): Bluehaus about 12 MB with an autoplay brand video; Norm about 15.7 MB; Neri&Hu about 13.5 MB with an autoplay film; Vincent Van Duysen about 25 MB for a single full-screen image page.
- **[S 2, S 4, S 15]** Loading friction observed: LW Design took about 6.7 s to DOMContentLoaded with 116 requests; Kristina Zanic shows a blank cream preloader for about 3 s; Waldo's home still rendered blank white in an 8 s screenshot.
- **[S 3, S 12, S 13]** Image-led sites can be light: Commune's home was about 242 KB, Studio McGee's first load about 0.4 MB, Roar's about 2 MB with DOMContentLoaded at 0.6 s.
- **[I]** Image-heavy design sites fail on performance mostly through a few repeated mistakes: giant hero images or autoplay video as LCP, lazy-loading the hero, missing dimensions, wrong `sizes`, and too many font files. All are avoidable with discipline rather than compromise on imagery.

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- **Performance budget (proposal):** LCP under 2.5 s on a mid-range phone over 4G; hero image delivered at mobile size in AVIF or WebP at roughly 200 KB or less **[I]**; no more than four font files on first load.
- **Implementation rules for the build phase:** hero as a server-rendered `<img>` with high fetch priority (in Next.js, `next/image` with priority) and never lazy-loaded; all other images lazy-loaded with explicit dimensions and correct `sizes`; fonts self-hosted via `next/font` with subsetting; no autoplay video as the LCP element.
- **Galleries:** a click or tap opens a larger view at least twice the inline size ([F 24]), keyboard and swipe accessible, no auto-advance ([F 30]).
- **Test before launch:** Lighthouse and field data once traffic exists; check every page at 375 px width with no horizontal scroll.

## 14. SEO for local high-intent clients in Dubai

**Findings**

- **[F 9]** Local ranking factors are relevance, distance and prominence (links, reviews); complete and accurate profile information helps; payment cannot buy ranking.
- **[F 10]** Google Business Profile guidelines: service-area businesses "should hide your business address"; a rented mailing address you do not operate from is not eligible; service-area businesses cannot list a virtual office "unless that office is staffed during business hours"; the name must match the real-world name without added keywords or taglines; use as few, as specific categories as possible.
- **[F 1]** Google's LocalBusiness structured data requires `name` and `address` (the physical location); recommends `telephone` (with country code), `url`, `geo`, `openingHoursSpecification`, `priceRange`; says to "Use the most specific LocalBusiness sub-type possible", allows arrays of types, does not support `additionalType`, and notes Organization guidelines also apply.
- **[F 11]** schema.org: `ProfessionalService` is a LocalBusiness subtype, but schema.org notes the general type for local businesses "was deprecated due to confusion with Service". There is no dedicated interior-designer type.
- **[F 2]** Google's Organization structured data has no required properties; recommended ones include `name`, `alternateName`, `legalName`, `url`, `logo`, `sameAs`, `address`, `contactPoint`, `email`, `telephone`, `foundingDate`; for a local business with a physical location, Google points to the most specific LocalBusiness type.
- **[F 3]** Self-serving reviews are not eligible for review rich results on LocalBusiness or Organization.
- **[F 4]** Google's spam policies define doorway abuse as pages "created to rank for specific, similar search queries", with the example of "pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page", and scaled content abuse includes using generative AI "to generate many pages without adding value for users".
- **[F 7]** Titles: unique, descriptive, concise, no keyword stuffing, brand briefly at start or end.
- **[F 8]** Language versions: use `hreflang` only for genuinely translated pages, with reciprocal links and absolute URLs; untranslated main content is treated as duplicate.
- **[F 6]** Image SEO: descriptive file names, alt text, images near relevant text, `og:image` or `primaryImageOfPage` for the preferred image.
- **[S 1, S 3, S 10]** Basic on-page SEO is often neglected even by well-known studios: Bluehaus's home title is "Bluehaus | Home"; Roar's is just "Roar" with an empty meta description; Vincent Van Duysen's title repeats the name twice and has no meta description.
- **[S 3, S 4, S 8, S 11, S 13, S 14, S 16]** Seven of the sixteen studios had no JSON-LD structured data at all. Waldo declares LocalBusiness with only name, URL and opening hours, no address **[S 15]**, which lacks a property Google lists as required **[F 1]**. Studio McGee marks up Organization with ContactPoint, PostalAddress and Person **[S 12]**.
- **[S 6]** Antonovich has the most extensive markup (HomeAndConstructionBusiness, GeoCoordinates, OfferCatalog, a Service per city) alongside a mega-menu of about 250 links including keyword pages such as "Best fit out companies in Dubai", and a blog post whose excerpt contains the literal text "Meta description:". **[I]** HomeAndConstructionBusiness fits a firm that builds; it would misdescribe a consultancy. The keyword-page sprawl is the pattern Google's doorway policy describes **[F 4]**.
- **[I]** High-intent local queries for ARCHIÓ look like "interior designer Dubai Marina", "office interior design JLT", "villa landscape design Arabian Ranches", "interior design consultant Dubai". The honest way to rank for them is a strong Business Profile, a page per service area of work, project pages that genuinely concern those communities, and useful local guides.

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- **Google Business Profile first.** If ARCHIÓ has a staffed office, list it; if not, register as a service-area business with the address hidden ([F 10]). Name exactly "Archió Interior Design Studio" (no "Best Interior Designer Dubai" suffix). Pick the most specific matching category.
- **Structured data now (no confirmed address):** `Organization` on the home page with `name` "Archió Interior Design Studio", `alternateName` ["ARCHIÓ", "Archio"], `url`, `logo`, `sameAs` (real profiles only), `email` and `telephone` (real only), `founder` as two `Person` entries with their roles, and `parentOrganization` Beracah Trading if the owner confirms the legal wording ([F 2]).
- **Structured data later (staffed address confirmed):** switch to a LocalBusiness type with a real `address`, `telephone`, `url` and opening hours ([F 1]). Plain `LocalBusiness` is the lowest-risk type; `ProfessionalService` validates as a subtype but schema.org flags the generic type as deprecated ([F 11]). Validate with the Rich Results Test.
- **Never** add Review or AggregateRating markup to ARCHIÓ's own pages ([F 3]).
- **No doorway pages.** Do not create "Interior designer in [community]" pages for twenty neighbourhoods ([F 4]). Communities appear where there is real content: the Dubai Marina, JLT and Arabian Ranches 2 project pages, and guides.
- **Titles (examples):** "Interior design consultancy in Dubai | Archió"; "Dubai Marina apartment, design visualisation | Archió"; "Office interior design and space planning | Archió" ([F 7]).
- **Arabic:** launch in English; add Arabic only as a full, human-reviewed translation with `hreflang`, never as machine-generated thin pages ([F 4], [F 8]).
- **Name, address and phone consistency** across the site, Business Profile, Instagram and LinkedIn ([F 9], [F 10]).

## 15. Current conventions that look generic or AI-generated

**Findings**

- **[S 1, S 3, S 4, S 5, S 15]** Recurring stock phrases and unbacked claims in the sample: "spaces that tell stories" in three variants (Roar, Waldo, Kristina Zanic); "award winning" with no award named (Bluehaus, Sneha Divias).
- **[S 3, S 4, S 6]** Count-up statistics strips (Roar, Kristina Zanic, Antonovich), two of which contradict themselves elsewhere on the same site.
- **[S 2, S 6]** Template and automation residue: LW Design's page source contains a raw `[instagram-feed...]` shortcode, placeholder social links and hidden "Lorem ipsum" (not visible to visitors); an Antonovich blog excerpt shows the literal text "Meta description:", and one post is titled "Antonovich Group Leads the List".
- **[S 1, S 5, S 7]** Auto-advancing photo slideshows with dot indicators as the hero (Bluehaus, Sneha Divias, Norm), the carousel pattern NN/g found reduces visibility **[F 30]**; a "Scroll Down" cue on Roar **[S 3]**; a 3-second blank preloader on Kristina Zanic **[S 4]**; a testimonial attributed only to "Liz N." on Studio McGee **[S 12]**.

- **[F 24]** Decorative imagery that carries no information is ignored and can make content look like advertising.
- **[F 4]**, **[F 5]** Google treats mass-produced, low-value AI pages as spam and asks that AI use be evident where readers would expect it.
- **[F 30]**, **[F 28]** Auto-rotating carousels and scrolljacking, both common "premium" effects, measurably frustrate users.
- **[I]** The project's design-taste guidance (the `design-taste-frontend` skill used for this research) catalogues patterns that recur in machine-generated and template sites: centred hero over a gradient, three or six identical feature cards, a small uppercase "eyebrow" label above every section, numbered section labels such as "01 / Services", "scroll to explore" cues, fake-precise statistics, em-dashes as decoration, decorative status dots, pills overlaid on images, invented photo-credit captions, filler verbs ("elevate", "seamless", "unleash"), and a default "premium" palette of cream or beige with brass or oxblood accents and espresso text.
- **[I]** Some of these appear on respected studios (Norm's slideshow), so they are not proof of machine-made work; they are signs of default choices, and a new studio cannot afford defaults the way an established one can.
- **[I]** Interior-design-specific tells: a full-screen slideshow hero headed "Luxury Interior Design Dubai"; "We create timeless spaces that reflect your personality"; count-up stats ("500+ projects, 15 years"); a testimonial carousel signed with first names; "Why choose us" with four icons; stock team photos; a floating WhatsApp bubble plus chat widget plus pop-up plus cookie bar stacked on load; and AI-generated rooms with warped joinery, melting furniture legs or impossible reflections.

**What this means for ARCHIÓ**

- **The palette warning applies directly.** ARCHIÓ's brand colours (oxblood, blush, sand) sit close to the palette the design-taste guidance flags as the generic default for premium brands. They are the client's real brand, so keep them, but avoid the default execution of them: not cream backgrounds everywhere with thin oxblood accents and italic serif headlines. Distinguish through composition: oxblood as a confident full field, imagery carrying warmth, the Optima-style display face rather than a trendy serif, and real plans and material boards that no template has.
- **Ban list for the design phase:** auto-advancing slideshow heroes; preloader screens; identical service cards; an eyebrow above every heading; numbered section labels; scroll cues; count-up stats; logo walls; anonymous or initial-only testimonials; stock people; decorative em-dashes; pills on images; invented photo credits; marquee strips; custom cursors; stacked pop-ups.
- **Launch hygiene:** search the built pages and source for placeholder text, unused shortcodes and stray CMS fields before launch ([S 2], [S 6], [F 23]).
- **Copy rule:** every sentence must say something specific to ARCHIÓ that a competitor could not paste onto its own site. If it could be pasted, cut it.
- **Image rule:** no generative-AI imagery presented as ARCHIÓ's work; any AI-assisted image disclosed ([F 5], [F 42]).
